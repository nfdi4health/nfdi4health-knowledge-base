# Submit a Word or Markdown contribution

This route is for NFDI4Health data stewards and service owners who prefer not to edit GitHub directly. **The coordinator handles technical integration; the named content steward retains responsibility for checking specialist information.**

## Step 1 — Select a template

Choose the template for a [service]({{ '/assets/contribution-templates/NFDI4Health_Service_Content_Template.docx' | relative_url }}), [module]({{ '/assets/contribution-templates/NFDI4Health_Module_Authoring_Template_V001.docx' | relative_url }}) or [life-cycle topic]({{ '/assets/contribution-templates/NFDI4Health_Lifecycle_Guidance_Template.docx' | relative_url }}) on the [main contribution page]({{ '/how_to_contribute' | relative_url }}). The module template follows the agreed V001 guide and explicitly permits partial submissions.

## Step 2 — Prepare the material

Include the page title, short description, target audience, actions users should take, relevant NFDI4Health service links, references, and any current limitations. If it is an update, identify the existing page URL and describe what changed. Provide the person/team who can verify technical accuracy and maintain the page.

You may supply a `.docx` with tracked changes or comments, or a `.md` file. If your content contains images, include the file, author, licence, attribution and an informative alternative-text suggestion. **Do not include identifiable health data or confidential attachments.**

## Step 3 — Submit to the coordination team

Send the editable files through the team's **approved internal submission channel**. The coordination team must insert the agreed contact address/channel before this page is published. For requests visible to the community, you may alternatively open a [GitHub Issue](https://github.com/nfdi4health/nfdi4health-knowledge-base/issues/new/choose) describing the proposed page, without uploading non-public documents.

Suggested message subject: **Knowledge Base contribution — [Service / Module / Life cycle] — [topic]**.

Provide: contributor name, team, topic, new/update status, a source URL, source/licence details and an answer to whether the steward can review subsequent changes.

## Step 4 — Review and revision

The coordinator records the contribution and assigns reviews. A **subject reviewer** checks technical and scientific accuracy; an **editorial reviewer** checks reader clarity, sources, FAIR context, accessibility and rights. One person may handle several tasks for minor corrections, but substantial new pages should have a review separate from their author. The content steward checks substantive revisions before publication.

Possible decisions: **Accept**, **Accept with minor changes**, **Revise and resubmit**, or **Do not publish (with reason)**. Review comments are returned in Word, a review log, or a GitHub pull request.

## Step 5 — Publication and maintenance

The coordinator or technical maintainer creates a branch and pull request, links the original submission in an appropriate approved location, and records contributor credit with consent. After review and successful site checks, an authorised maintainer merges and checks the public URL. The content steward is contacted when a service change makes an update necessary and participates in periodic checks; a six-month cycle is a **suggested default, not an approved project requirement**.

**Never treat emailing a document as automatic approval or publication.**
