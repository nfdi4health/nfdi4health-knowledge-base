Effective biomedical research data management starts **before the first participant is recruited or the first dataset is accessed**. At the planning stage, researchers decide which data are needed, how they will be collected or reused, who is responsible for them, how participants' privacy will be protected, and how the resulting data and documentation can remain findable and useful.

NFDI4Health provides practical planning resources for **clinical, epidemiological and public health research**, including dedicated **Research Data Management Plan (DMP) templates**, a health research metadata schema, study discovery services, guidance and support. These resources help research teams make FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable) decisions while recognising that access to personal health data may need to remain restricted.

## Learning objectives

After using this guidance, you should be able to:

- identify the data management decisions that must be made before a biomedical study begins;
- choose and begin completing an appropriate NFDI4Health DMP template;
- identify relevant NFDI4Health services and standards for your planned workflow;
- plan for documentation, data quality, data protection, secure access, preservation and responsible reuse; and
- recognise when institutional experts and the NFDI4Health Helpdesk should be consulted.

## What is research data management planning?

Research data management planning is the process of agreeing and documenting how research data will be handled **before, during and after** a study. Its central output is a **Data Management Plan (DMP)**: a formal, *living document* describing the data, their generation or acquisition, their processing, protection, documentation, sharing and long-term retention.

A DMP is not identical to a study protocol. A **study protocol** explains the scientific objectives and how the study will be carried out; the **DMP** explains how the resulting data and supporting documentation will be managed. Both documents should be consistent, but neither replaces the other.

In biomedical research, planning is especially important because datasets may contain directly or indirectly identifiable health information, involve several institutions, combine multiple data sources, and be subject to ethical, contractual and legal requirements.

## Why plan early?

Early planning helps teams to:

- **Protect participants:** identify the applicable data protection requirements, access restrictions and safeguards before data are collected or transferred.
- **Improve data quality:** agree on definitions, validated instruments, quality checks, units and coding schemes before inconsistencies arise.
- **Make data FAIR:** capture study-level metadata, variable definitions, provenance and intended access conditions from the outset.
- **Support collaboration:** document roles, responsibilities, data-sharing agreements and arrangements among participating centres.
- **Avoid avoidable costs:** estimate storage, computing, documentation, stewardship, quality assurance and preservation resources.
- **Enable responsible reuse:** decide what information may become publicly discoverable even when participant-level data cannot be made openly accessible.

Planning is iterative. Revisit the DMP when the protocol changes, new data sources are added, access conditions change or a study enters a new phase.

## NFDI4Health Research Data Management Plans: the starting point

NFDI4Health has developed **three DMP templates** for health research, supported by an accompanying **instructions-for-use document**. Select the template that best matches the study and any requirements of your institution or funder.

| NFDI4Health template | When to use it | Planning emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Research Data Management Plan – Epidemiological Studies** | Cohort, population-based and other epidemiological research projects | Study populations, repeated observations, multiple data sources, documentation, linkage, sharing and long-term stewardship |
| **Research Data Management Plan – Clinical Studies** | Clinical research, including interventional and non-interventional studies | Protocol-aligned data collection, responsibilities, clinical documentation, quality control, regulatory and ethical requirements |
| **Short DMP – DFG Proposal** | Early project design and grant applications | A concise initial account of how research data will be handled, developed into a fuller plan as the project progresses |

**Access the official resources:** [NFDI4Health DMP templates and instructions](https://www.nfdi4health.de/en/service/guidelines/researchdatamanagementplans.html). The page provides links to all three templates and the accompanying instructions. The shorter template may also be used as a preliminary DMP alongside a research proposal, where appropriate.

### How to prepare your NFDI4Health DMP

1. **Choose the relevant template** and check whether your funder, sponsor or institution has additional requirements.
2. **Describe the study and data**: objectives, design, population, sources, data types, estimated volume and planned outputs.
3. **Agree on responsibilities** with investigators, data managers, data-holding organisations, IT services and other relevant partners.
4. **Document safeguards**: applicable legal bases and permissions, ethical approvals, access roles, storage, backups, confidentiality and planned de-identification measures.
5. **Plan FAIR documentation and reuse**: metadata, data dictionaries, terminologies, quality checks, identifiers, data access arrangements and preservation.
6. **Review the draft together** with institutional research data management, data protection, legal, ethics and information-security contacts as necessary.
7. **Assign an owner and review dates** so the DMP is updated as the study progresses, rather than filed away after project approval.

### Preparing a DMP with RDMO

**Research Data Management Organiser (RDMO)** is a tool for creating and maintaining structured DMPs. Through the **DMP4NFDI** collaboration, the NFDI4Health *Epidemiological Studies* template has been implemented in RDMO. Work during 2026 also includes training materials and preparation for RDMO implementations of the clinical and short templates.

This development complements the official downloadable templates; it does not mean that every researcher already has access to a public NFDI4Health RDMO instance. Check available access and guidance with the [NFDI4Health Helpdesk](https://www.nfdi4health.de/en/helpdesk.html).

## What should a biomedical DMP cover?

The topics below provide a practical guide for completing and reviewing a DMP. They reflect NFDI4Health training on data management planning; **they are a planning checklist, not a replacement for the questions in the official templates**.

| Planning topic | Questions to resolve and document |
| --- | --- |
| **Project and responsibilities** | What is the study design and purpose? Who leads data management, quality assurance, security, documentation and preservation? Which organisations hold or process the data? |
| **Data description and sources** | Will data be newly collected, extracted from electronic health records, obtained from registries or reused from existing studies? What formats, data volumes and sensitivity levels are expected? |
| **Data collection and organisation** | Which instruments, case report forms, questionnaires, electronic data capture systems, naming conventions and version-control procedures will be used? |
| **Metadata and documentation** | Which study metadata, data dictionaries, codebooks, processing logs and provenance records must be created? Which metadata standards and controlled terminologies apply? |
| **Data quality and analysis** | Which completeness, consistency and plausibility checks are planned? How will errors, exclusions and derived variables be recorded? How will analysis code and decisions remain reproducible? |
| **Law, ethics and confidentiality** | What legal bases and permissions apply? What is the relevant GDPR Article 9 condition? Which ethics and other approvals are necessary? What are the intended purposes, access limits and de-identification safeguards? |
| **Storage and security** | Where will identifiable data and pseudonymisation keys be stored? Who has access? How will encryption, backups, recovery, logging and secure transfers be arranged? |
| **Discovery, access and sharing** | Which study descriptions and documents can be published? Who approves access to underlying data? What agreements, conditions and reuse restrictions apply? |
| **Preservation, deletion and resources** | Which files and documentation must be retained, for how long and under which applicable rules? How will they remain interpretable? Who funds stewardship, infrastructure and eventual secure deletion? |

**Important:** Retention periods and requirements depend on the type of study, the applicable legal and regulatory framework, funder conditions and institutional policy. Do not assume one universal retention period for all biomedical datasets.

**What the services do not do:** The Health Study Hub is primarily a mechanism for making studies and related resources **discoverable through metadata**. It does not require public release of identifiable or participant-level health data. The original data-holding organisation retains responsibility for access decisions and the secure management of its research data. Similarly, a DMP template supports good planning but does not constitute legal authorisation, ethical approval or regulatory clearance.

## Applying FAIR principles from the planning stage

FAIR should be designed into the project, rather than added after analysis:

- **Findable:** plan rich study and dataset metadata, stable identifiers, and appropriate registration or publication of non-sensitive information in the Health Study Hub.
- **Accessible:** document who can obtain which resources, by what process, and under what conditions; public metadata can coexist with restricted access to underlying health data.
- **Interoperable:** use the NFDI4Health Metadata Schema and suitable standard terminologies, documented formats and variable definitions from the beginning.
- **Reusable:** retain contextual documentation, provenance, quality information, permissions, permitted reuse purposes and long-term responsibilities.

For more background, see the [NFDI4Health Metadata Schema](https://www.nfdi4health.de/en/service/metadata-schema.html) and [publication policy](https://www.nfdi4health.de/en/service/health-study-hub/publication-policy-and-metadata-schema.html).

## Planning for sensitive health data

Before collecting or reusing personal health information, work with the responsible institutional experts to establish the applicable safeguards. In particular:

1. **Clarify governance:** identify relevant parties and responsibilities, including the data controller(s), processors where applicable, and data-holding organisations.
2. **Confirm lawful processing:** document the applicable GDPR Article 6 legal basis and Article 9 condition, and any further national, sector-specific, contractual or ethical requirements. Consent to take part in research and the legal basis for processing personal data should not automatically be treated as the same thing.
3. **Apply data minimisation:** collect and retain only information that is necessary for the defined research purposes.
4. **Plan pseudonymisation and separation:** describe how identifiers, linkage files and pseudonymisation keys will be managed, including access restrictions. **Pseudonymised data remain personal data** when individuals can still be identified by reasonably available means.
5. **Plan security and disclosure controls:** define secure storage and transfer, access authorisation, audit trails, incident response and safeguards against re-identification.
6. **Define future access and retention:** record which data may be reused, the review or application pathway, and the applicable retention and deletion requirements.

These are research planning considerations, **not legal advice or a declaration that a proposed processing activity is permitted**. Seek formal advice and approvals from the responsible institution where required.

## Example: planning a multicentre epidemiological study

*Illustrative scenario:* A research team wishes to study associations between lifestyle factors and the incidence of type 2 diabetes using questionnaires, clinical measurements and existing electronic health records at several institutions.

| Planning decision | Example of an NFDI4Health-supported action |
| --- | --- |
| **Determine whether comparable data already exist** | Search the Health Study Hub for relevant cohorts, variables, questionnaires and reuse conditions before establishing new collection. |
| **Write the data strategy** | Start with the NFDI4Health *Epidemiological Studies* DMP template; record data sources, participating centres, responsibilities, security and preservation arrangements. |
| **Agree on common descriptions** | Use the Metadata Schema to identify study metadata fields; plan a shared data dictionary and controlled terminology for outcomes and exposures. |
| **Define the data quality workflow** | Specify missingness, plausible ranges, inconsistent dates and cross-site coding checks; assess whether dataquieR fits the planned workflow. |
| **Choose an analysis and access model** | Assess whether individual-level data must remain at each institution and whether DataSHIELD or another distributed approach is feasible. Obtain the necessary institutional approvals. |
| **Plan later discovery and reuse** | Decide which study metadata and supporting documents can be made publicly discoverable through the Health Study Hub, while keeping access to personal health data under institutional control. |

The result should be an agreed, documented plan **before** the participating institutions begin extracting, collecting or transferring the study data.

## Planning checklist

Use this checklist before data collection or a new data-access application starts:

- [ ] &nbsp;&nbsp;A suitable NFDI4Health DMP template has been selected and a first draft prepared.
- [ ] &nbsp;&nbsp;Study objectives, data sources, expected outputs and data types are described.
- [ ] &nbsp;&nbsp;Responsibilities and contacts for data stewardship, security and approvals are recorded.
- [ ] &nbsp;&nbsp;Appropriate ethical, legal and data protection review arrangements are identified.
- [ ] &nbsp;&nbsp;Study metadata, identifiers, data dictionaries and terminology choices are planned.
- [ ] &nbsp;&nbsp;Data collection, validation, processing and analysis workflows are documented.
- [ ] &nbsp;&nbsp;Institutional storage, backup, encryption and access procedures are agreed.
- [ ] &nbsp;&nbsp;Study discovery, secondary data access and possible federated analysis have been considered.
- [ ] &nbsp;&nbsp;Sharing, publication, preservation, retention and deletion conditions are documented.
- [ ] &nbsp;&nbsp;The required personnel, infrastructure and financial resources are budgeted.
- [ ] &nbsp;&nbsp;A DMP owner and review milestones are assigned.

## When should the DMP be updated?

Treat the DMP as a living record of project decisions. Useful review points include **before recruitment or data access**, after protocol amendments, when adding new centres or data sources, before analysing or sharing data, and at study close-out or transfer to long-term preservation. Record the date, responsible person and reason for significant changes.
