# Health Study Hub

## What is this service?

The Health Study Hub is an NFDI4Health service that supports the FAIR publication, discovery, and reuse of metadata from clinical, epidemiological, and public health studies.

The platform serves two primary user groups:

- **Data producers**, such as researchers and data-holding organisations, who wish to publish structured study metadata and related documentation.
- **Data consumers**, such as researchers seeking reusable datasets, study documentation, questionnaires, instruments, or metadata for secondary analysis.

The Health Study Hub improves discoverability of health research resources while respecting legal, ethical, and governance constraints for sensitive health data.

Importantly, patient-level personal health data are **not openly published** through the platform. Instead, metadata, documentation, and information about access conditions are made discoverable to support responsible reuse.

## Why use this service?

The Health Study Hub helps researchers and data stewards to:

- improve discoverability of study metadata
- support FAIR publication practices
- document study resources consistently
- identify existing datasets relevant for reuse
- compare variables and instruments across studies
- improve metadata interoperability
- connect data producers with potential data users
- support transparent access pathways for sensitive health data
- ## Key Features

### Study Discovery

Researchers can search across clinical, epidemiological, and public health studies using structured metadata and search filters.

This supports discovery of:

- study descriptions
- datasets
- questionnaires
- codebooks
- data dictionaries
- instruments
- supplementary study documentation

Depending on metadata completeness, exploration can extend down to variable or questionnaire-item level.

---

### Metadata Publication

Data producers can publish descriptive metadata about their studies and associated resources.

This may include:

- study characteristics
- project documentation
- protocols
- questionnaires
- data dictionaries
- access information
- preservation metadata

Metadata publication improves visibility without requiring open publication of sensitive participant-level data.

---

### FAIR Metadata Exposure

The platform supports FAIR-aligned publication by making metadata:

- machine-readable
- searchable
- structured
- reusable for discovery and interoperability purposes

This improves responsible reuse while respecting governance restrictions.

---

### Variable-Level Exploration

One distinguishing feature of the Health Study Hub is fine-grained metadata exploration.

Users may compare:

- variables
- questionnaires
- instruments
- study documentation

This supports harmonisation planning and secondary analysis preparation.

---

### Repository Aggregation

The platform integrates and harmonises information from public research registries and repositories, including:

- DRKS
- ClinicalTrials.gov
- WHO ICTRP

This reduces fragmentation and improves visibility of distributed health research resources.

---

### Submission Pathways

Metadata can be contributed through multiple routes:

**Manual submission**
via graphical web interface

**Automated submission**
via API integration

**Assisted submission**
with support from trained data stewards

This flexibility lowers barriers for FAIR metadata publication.

---

### Data Steward Support

Data stewards support researchers when:

- metadata mapping is complex
- harmonisation is needed
- structured submission requires assistance
- FAIR publication planning is unclear

This improves metadata quality and contributor onboarding.

---

## How this service supports the research lifecycle

### Study Planning

Researchers can identify existing studies before launching new projects.

This supports:

- avoiding duplication
- identifying reusable resources
- reviewing comparable questionnaires
- planning harmonised data collection

---

### Study Setup and Documentation

During study preparation, teams can structure metadata consistently and prepare documentation for future discoverability.

This supports:

- metadata planning
- documentation standardisation
- interoperability readiness

---

### Data Publication

After study execution, structured metadata and related documentation can be published through the platform.

This improves:

- visibility
- discoverability
- FAIR compliance

---

### Controlled Sharing and Access Discovery

Although participant-level health data remain protected, metadata can describe:

- data availability
- access pathways
- governance conditions
- reuse eligibility

This supports responsible access discovery.

---

### Reuse and Secondary Analysis

Researchers seeking existing resources can identify relevant studies and prepare reuse workflows.

This supports:

- comparative studies
- secondary analysis
- harmonisation
- evidence synthesis
